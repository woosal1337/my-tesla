# My Tesla mobile UI research

Research date: 2026-10-17. Checked against Expo SDK 58 (React Native 0.88, React 19.3, expo-router 58.0.23), the Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Material Design 3, WCAG 2.2, and the package registries.

## Question

Which navigation, components, charts, and maps give a native iOS and Android app that follows the Tesla style of the web dashboard, stays small, and is accessible?

## Navigation

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Tabs | Five native tabs: Overview, Drives, Charging, Insights, Settings | Apple asks for the minimum number of tabs and, in general, three to five on iPhone. Material 3 and the Android navigation bar allow three to five destinations, and Android native tabs allow at most five. Battery, Stats, and Places already share one period control on the web, so they form Insights. |
| Settings | A tab, not a modal | Settings now changes the web Settings, with a picker for each choice. A tab keeps that stack of screens, and the gear button on Overview is gone. Five is the limit, so a new destination needs a decision record. |
| Choice lists | A pushed screen with one row for each option and a check mark on the chosen one. It stays open after a choice. | The Settings pattern of iOS: the check mark moves at once, and Back returns. A choice saves at once, with the old value back when the server refuses it. |
| Settings groups | The server names each group of the app after the screen that its settings change: "Insights" holds the default period, "Overview" holds "Car image" and "Last drive and charge", and "Status and Location" holds "Live status", "Today", "Tires", and "Location". The web keeps one group "Overview", because its Overview page holds all the cards. The app and the web share the keys and the labels. | Android says "Group settings in smaller relevant groups" with headings between them, and asks for settings that are "well-organized, predictable". Apple groups related settings, and a grouped list separates its groups with headers. WCAG 2.4.6 asks that headings "describe topic or purpose". One group "Overview and Status" did not name the Location screen. The label "Location" repeats a word of its group title, which Android advises against. It stays the same as the web label. |
| Search in a long list | `Stack.SearchBar` on a choice list with more than 12 options, such as the time zone | The native search field of the header. SDK 55 and later replace `headerSearchBarOptions` with this component. |
| Stacks | A `Stack` inside each tab folder, with `unstable_settings = { anchor: "index" }` | `initialRouteName` is gone in SDK 58. The anchor puts the list under a deep link, so Back returns to it. |
| Status bar on a screen without a header | `Screen withoutHeader` draws an opaque strip in the page background over the top safe-area inset, on iOS and on Android. The strip ignores touches and comes after the ScrollView, so the ScrollView stays the first child. On Android, the content also starts below the inset. The Overview on a phone, Connect, and the two states of `CarProvider` use it. The Overview on an iPad has a navigation bar and passes `coverStatusBar`: `Screen` draws the same strip where the screen lies under the bar, which `headerCoversScreen()` decides (iOS 26 and later for a bar without a large title). | On iPadOS 27 the navigation bar of the Overview did not hide the content under the status bar. At the text size `accessibility-large` in light mode, the car name and the time of the last drive showed through the clock and the date. Below iOS 26 that bar is opaque and the screen starts below it, so a strip there would cover the top of the content. Apple says: "Obscure content under the status bar." Apple prefers a scroll edge effect, and iOS 27 adds its automatic soft effect to the Overview, but the blur still showed the dark car behind the clock in light mode. The hard style of `UIScrollEdgeEffect` needs iOS 26, and the app supports iOS 16.4 and Android, so the app needs a strip in any case. `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior` works on iOS only. Android 15 and later draw every app edge to edge, so the first row of the Overview sat under the status bar on an Android 16 emulator until the inset was added. Android protects the status bar with the background color of the pane. With the strip, the status bar text always sits on the page background, so its contrast does not depend on the content (WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.3). The car name stays directly below the status bar, as [decision 0025](../docs/decisions/0025-the-tesla-app-layout.md) shows it. A solid strip needs no gradient, so the stage glow stays the only one. Rejected: a navigation bar on the phone, because it moves the name down by about 53 pt. The hard scroll edge effect, because it needs iOS 26 and still blurs. |
| Overview header on the iPad | On an iPad, the `index` of the Overview stack keeps its navigation bar (`overviewHeaderShown` in `src/navigation/header.ts`). On iPadOS 27 the bar sits behind the top tab bar and shows no title. The car name stays below the tab bar in every state. The bar does not hide the content under the status bar, so the Overview passes `coverStatusBar` and gets the status bar strip of the row above. The phone and Android keep the Overview without a header. | The car name went under the tab bar after a return from Status or Location, by the tab or by the back button. The Overview stack hid the `UINavigationBar` for `index` and showed it for the detail screens. After the bar hid again, UIKit gave the scroll view a top inset of the status bar only, and a second press, a tab switch, or a pull to refresh kept it. Drives keeps its bar on the root screen and did not have the problem. When the bar never hides in the stack, UIKit keeps the inset of the tab bar, as on the other four tabs. The name now sits 10 pt higher than before at rest, and at the same place after each return. Apple puts the iPad tab bar near the top of the screen. Apple forum reports show that the safe area of iOS 26 can go stale after a bar change. Rejected: a manual inset for the tab bar, because JavaScript cannot read the inset after UIKit loses it. |
| Active tab pressed again | Native tabs pop the stack, or scroll the root screen to the top. On iOS, `Screen` completes the scroll with `useScrollToRest()` in `src/hooks/`. The hook keeps the offset of the first drag after mount that starts below zero, which is the offset at rest with the large title at full size. An offset of zero or more means that the page moved before the first drag, by VoiceOver, a hardware keyboard, or the keyboard inset, so the hook keeps no offset and does nothing. When the active tab is pressed again, `useScrollToTop` of `expo-router` calls the hook, and the hook scrolls to that offset. The ScrollView sets `scrollToOverflowEnabled`, because the offset at rest is negative. | `rnscreens_scrollToTop` of `react-native-screens` 4.30 scrolls to minus `adjustedContentInset.top`. With a collapsed large title, that inset is the height of the small bar, so the scroll stopped there. The title stayed small on Drives, Charging, Insights, and Settings, and on the iPad it disappeared. A programmatic scroll does not bring a large title back on its own (Apple forum). JavaScript cannot read `adjustedContentInset`, and `scrollTo` clamps a negative offset unless `scrollToOverflowEnabled` is on. React Navigation documents a wrapped ref with its own `scrollToTop` for a custom offset. `useScrollToTop` scrolls only a focused screen that is the first route of its stack, so a first press on another tab and a pop do not scroll. Before the first drag the page is at rest, so the hook does nothing. Android has no large title and its native tabs scroll to the top, so the hook does nothing there. Limit: a change of the text size while a screen is mounted changes the height of the large title, and a resize of an iPad window between the top tab bar and the bottom tab bar changes the top inset. In both cases the kept offset is wrong until the screen mounts again. Rejected: a patch of `rnscreens_scrollToTop`, because a native patch needs upkeep and UIKit has no public value for the height of the large bar. A remount of the ScrollView, because it loses the state and has no animation. |
| Headers | Native large titles on the list screens, a small title on the detail screens | The ScrollView must be the first child for the large title to collapse. iOS 26 makes headers transparent with Liquid Glass by default, so the app adds no blur. |
| Header fonts | `headerTitleStyle` and `headerLargeTitleStyle` set Inter | iOS 26 headers ignore the theme fonts in SDK 58. |
| Lists | A ScrollView with sticky day headings | Native tabs scroll a ScrollView to the top and pop the stack when the active tab is pressed again. They do not do this for a FlatList. On iOS, `Screen` completes the scroll, so a large title returns to its full size (see "Active tab pressed again"). A list holds at most 50 rows. |
| Day headings under the bar | Drives, Charging, and the Report render through `DayList`. Where the navigation bar covers the list, `DayList` gives the ScrollView `StickyHeading` as its `StickyHeaderComponent`. `StickyHeading` does the interpolation of the React Native wrapper on the scroll offset plus `useAnimatedHeaderHeight()` of `expo-router/native-stack`, so the pinned heading sits at the lower edge of the bar. `headerCoversScreen()` in `sticky-geometry.ts` is true on iOS 26 and later, and for a large title on an older iOS. Elsewhere the ScrollView keeps the default wrapper. The heading keeps the page background of `SectionHeading`, so the rows do not show through it. | `Screen` sets `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior="automatic"`, so UIKit adds the bar height as an adjusted inset, and the offset at rest is minus the bar height. The default wrapper (`ScrollViewStickyHeader`) starts to pin a heading when the offset reaches the layout `y` of the heading, which is the top of the scroll view frame. `ScrollView` adds only the `contentInset` prop to its scroll value. On iOS 26 and later, `react-native-screens` 4.30 always lays a screen out under the bar (`screensExtendUnderOpaqueNavigationBar`). Before iOS 26 it does this only for a translucent header, and a large title makes the header translucent. So the heading stuck behind the status bar and the bar, about 116 pt too high on an iPhone 17 Pro. The animated header height follows the bar on the native thread when the large title collapses: `RNSScreen.mm` computes it in `calculateHeaderHeightIsModal`. The scroll value is 0 until the first scroll event, while the true offset is minus the bar height, so `StickyHeading` pins nothing before that event. The heading is one line, about 36 pt at the default size, and a row is at least 64 pt high, so a pinned heading never hides a focused row completely (WCAG 2.2 rule 2.4.11). A pinned heading follows the finger and is not an animation, so Reduce Motion does not change it. Apple asks for a clear separation for pinned table headers under the scroll edge effect, and Android pins the headers of grouped data too. The web pins `DayHeading` under its header with the page background. The Report put its headings in a nested view before, so they did not stick. Rejected: an opaque header, because the large title needs a translucent header and the Liquid Glass bar would go away on these screens only. A fixed `contentInset` with `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior="never"`, because it breaks the collapse of the large title and the tab bar inset. A wrapper around `ScrollViewStickyHeader`, because it needs a deep import from `react-native/Libraries`, which has no types in React Native 0.88. `useHeaderHeight()`, because expo-router delays the number by 100 ms on a large title screen, so the heading sticks 52 pt too low for a moment after a fast scroll. A second heading drawn from JavaScript scroll events, because it lags by a frame and has no push between two headings. `SectionList` or FlashList, because they use the same wrapper, and native tabs do not scroll them to the top. |
| Day total in the heading | A day heading of Drives, Charging, and the Report shows the date and the total of the day in one line: "Wed 21 Oct · 4 trips · 64 km", "Tue 20 Oct · +18.9 kWh", or both parts on a Report day with trips and charges. The server sends the parts in `total` of each section, in the units of the person, and `headingLine()` joins them after the date with `summaryLine()`. The heading keeps the style, the header role, and the one text element of `SectionHeading`. With an older server, `total` is absent and the heading shows only the date. | Apple asks for a header that gives the context of a list and for short item text that does not truncate, and puts the most important information first, so the date stays first and each part stays short (Apple HIG lists and tables, writing). WCAG 2.2 rule 2.4.6 asks that a heading describes its topic and says that a heading does not need to be long. `summaryLine()` keeps the words of each part together, so at a large text size the heading wraps only between parts and never cuts (WCAG 2.2 rules 1.4.4 and 1.4.10, Apple Larger Text). The web shows the same parts through `DotList`, and both read them from `src/lib/day-total.ts`. `total` is optional in the contract because the app and a self-hosted server have separate releases, and `listShape` stays loose for the same reason. A Report day counts only its rows, so a charge inside a trip does not add to the energy part. Rejected: the total inside `title`, because one text can break "64" from "km" and the React keys use `title`. A right-aligned total, because it needs a second style and a layout that fails at a large text size. A cost in the Charging heading, because the summary line of the list shows the cost and the heading stays short. |
| Long list in a panel | The lock history shows 5 rows and a secondary **Show N more** button that extends the list in place; **Show fewer** folds it back | Apple and Material both prefer progressive disclosure over a menu for more of the same rows, and the rows stay in the scroll view, so VoiceOver reads them in order. The web uses a native `details` element for the same panel. |
| Overview structure | The car on a stage with callouts, the name with a chevron that switches the car, a row of quick actions, then a menu of rows with an icon, a title, and a short summary. On a wide screen the menu is a sidebar on the left and the car on the right. The right pane is as high as the sidebar, the stage fills the pane above the quick actions, and the callouts and the car sit in its center. | Ege asked for the layout of Ian Zelbo's Tesla app concept (2026-10-22). The Tesla app itself stacks the car, the quick actions, and a list of sections on a phone; Apple's sidebar guidance puts a persistent sidebar on the leading edge of a wide window and a stacked layout in a compact width, and Material 3 uses a navigation rail or drawer at 840 dp and wider. The rows follow the Material 3 two-line list item (leading icon, headline, supporting text, 64 dp min height) and Apple's sidebar row (icon and title, the selected row filled). |
| Callouts | A small uppercase label over a bold value, three at most, with no line. The callouts sit in the layout flow above the car: the top callout in the center, then the left and the right callout in one row at the 20 pt screen margin. The row wraps, so at a large text size the right callout moves under the left one. The label uses `mutedForeground`. A value has no line limit and wraps. Values come formatted from the server. Since [decision 0028](../docs/decisions/0028-the-car-fills-the-stage.md). Content since 2026-11-16: the battery and the range on the left, with the warning tone at 20% or less; the lock and Sentry Mode on top, only with the MQTT feed; the power and the limit of a charge on the right, else the charge port, else the odometer. Without the feed the stage has two callouts. | The concept's callouts name a part and its state. Three keep the stage readable at 390 pt. The lines of 24 pt stopped in empty space, and the render has no fixed anchor points across models, so a line cannot reach a part of the car ([decision 0025](../docs/decisions/0025-the-tesla-app-layout.md)). Apple keeps content inside the system margins, and every other element of the Overview keeps 20 pt, so the callouts do too. The label with `subtle` had 3.3:1 in light and 3.8:1 in dark, below the 4.5:1 of WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.3 for 12 pt text. `mutedForeground` gives 6.5:1 in light and 7.4:1 in dark. Text in the flow grows the stage and never covers the car, and the label breaks only between words (WCAG 2.2 rules 1.4.4 and 1.4.10). A screen reader reads each callout as one element. The "Last seen" callout repeated the age in the title, and the word "Charging" as a value repeated the state, so both went away. The low battery glyph of the title went away too, so the left callout carries the low warning. The value is always text, so the tone never carries the meaning alone (WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.1). |
| One place for each value | Each fact of the Overview shows in one place: the state and the age in the title, the battery and the range in the left callout, the lock on top, the charge or the odometer on the right. A menu summary gives a fact of the screen that it opens and that the stage does not show: the last drive, the last charge, the cold battery, the health and the capacity, the distance and the efficiency of the default period, the live values that the stage does not show. The server builds the summaries, and a test checks that no part of the title, the callouts, and the summaries repeats. [Decision 0025](../docs/decisions/0025-the-tesla-app-layout.md) point 7 has the table. | The battery showed three times and the odometer two times. Apple's layout guidance says: "An interface with too much content and too many choices makes it harder to find information quickly". Apple's writing guidance says: "Check each word to be sure it needs to be there." and asks to "put the most important information first". Apple's lists guidance says: "Keep item text succinct so row content is comfortable to read." Material 3 gives a two-line list item a headline and supporting text, so the summary supports the title with its own fact. The Battery and Stats summaries use the same helpers as the pages that they open, so the numbers match. The Stats summary uses the default period of the person, because the Stats page has no calendar month. Rejected: the battery in the title and not in the callout, because the callout keeps the level and the range together under a label. |
| Stage glow | One radial gradient under the car in `CarStage`, drawn with `react-native-svg`: the foreground color at 6% in light and 9% in dark, to the same color at 0%. The ellipse is 1.08 car widths wide and 0.56 car heights high, and its center is 12% of the car height above the lower edge of the car. | The only gradient in the app. The concept lights the car from below; a flat card looked like a broken image. The web hero uses the same colors and opacities. Its ellipse is 4.2 times as wide as it is high, and the app ellipse is 4.5 times for the Model Y. The old gradient went from `card` to `background`, so it was almost invisible in dark and did not show in light. The ellipse stays inside the 20 pt bleed of the stage: a glow of 1.24 car widths, as on the web, showed a hard edge where the stage clips it next to the iPad sidebar. The glow is decorative, so WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.11 sets no contrast for it. SVG is already bundled for the charts, so no package. |
| Car on the stage | The server sends `car.imageFrame`, the part of the render that the car fills, in fractions of the image width and height. `carSlot()` in `car-frame.ts` turns it into a slot with the aspect ratio of the car and the position of the 16:9 image around it. The car spans the content width, the screen width minus the two 20 pt margins, up to 440 pt. With an older server, or a frame that is not inside the image, the slot is the whole render. | The car covers 45% to 69% of the render width, different for each generation ([car images](car-images.md#where-the-car-sits-in-the-render)), so one fixed scale cuts one car or leaves another small. The app cannot read the pixels without a native package. The answer comes from outside the app, so `carSlot()` checks the four numbers. Read [decision 0028](../docs/decisions/0028-the-car-fills-the-stage.md). |
| Stage placeholder | The breathing T-mark (`TeslaMark kind="breathe"`) in the center of the car slot until `onLoad` of `expo-image`. The slot has its final size from the frame, so nothing moves when the car shows. `onError` removes the slot. A new image URL, such as another car, starts the load again. | Apple asks to show placeholder graphics at once and to replace them when the content arrives. The `placeholder` prop of `expo-image` takes only an image, so the mark is a view under the image. The mark already hides itself from a screen reader and stops its motion with Reduce Motion. |
| Quick actions | Four icon buttons in a row (Timeline, Report, Year, Maps), 48 pt circles with a caption | The concept's Flash, Honk, Start, Vent row. The app is read-only, so the row holds the four places people open most. Apple: 44 pt targets; the caption gives each icon a name. |
| Car switcher | The car name with a chevron; a press expands the other cars as rows under it. Each row has two lines: the name, and the battery and the state of that car ("70% · Online"). The row reads the Overview answer of that car with `useOverview` when the list opens. The second line keeps its height with a space while it loads, so the row does not jump. The label of the row says the name, the battery, and the state. | The concept's name with a chevron. An inline list works the same on iOS and Android, needs no menu library, and reads as a disclosure (`accessibilityState.expanded`). The segmented control went away because the names did not fit at three cars. Apple says: "Use a disclosure control to hide details until they're relevant." and "Establish a clear relationship between the control and the expanded choices". The battery and the state help to choose the car, so the expanded rows show them. Material 3 lists put a headline over supporting text. WCAG 2.2 rule 4.1.2 asks that the name of a control is available to assistive technology, so the label says the same facts as the row. TanStack Query manages its cache "based on query keys", so the row uses the same key as the Overview of that car, and the chosen car shows at once. Rows mount only while the list is open, so the app adds no poll while it is closed. Rejected: the battery and the state in `/api/cars.json`, because the app stores that answer on the phone, so the values go stale. A list of the other cars in the Overview answer, because it adds a query for each car to every poll for a list that is seldom open. |
| Links from an alert | `router.navigate` to a known route only, after the car is chosen | The notification data comes from outside the app, so the app accepts only the routes in its own list. |
| More screens of a tab | Timeline and Report in Drives, Charging insights in Charging, and the year in review in Stats open from a `LinkRow` with a chevron at the top of the list, not from header buttons. | The Apple HIG uses a disclosure indicator for a row that opens another view, and Material 3 lists use a trailing icon for navigation. A header button would need `Stack.Toolbar`, which needs `@expo/ui` on Android. Five tabs stay the limit. |
| Year picker | A compact segmented control with the five newest years that have data | Apple suggests about five segments. An owner with more years sees the newest five; the web lists all. |
| Report range | The four presets of the web (7 days, 30 days, this month, this year) as a segmented control. No custom date range in the app. | A date picker needs a native package. The web keeps the free range. |
| Exports | `expo-file-system` downloads the CSV or GPX with the phone token into the cache, and `expo-sharing` opens the share sheet. | React Native `Share` shares only text on Android. A browser link would not carry the token. |
| Pairing link from the camera | The QR code is a `mytesla://connect?…` link. When the system camera opens it, Connect shows the server address with **Connect** and **Cancel**, and claims the code only after a press. | Expo Router opens a deep link as the route with its query as search params. A link can come from any app or web page, so the app never acts on it alone: the press, the single-use code, and the approval on the web with the matching number keep a stray link from signing the phone in somewhere else. |
| Two controls | One full-width segmented control for Battery, Stats, and Places. The period is a compact control in the section that it changes. | Apple uses a segmented control for closely related views. Two stacked controls of the same weight confuse. `Stack.Toolbar` would need `@expo/ui` on Android. |

## Components

| Topic | Decision | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pressables | `Pressable`, with `accessibilityRole`, an explicit `accessibilityLabel`, and `accessibilityState` for disabled, busy, and selected | React Native docs recommend Pressable over the Touchable components. A busy button replaces its text with a spinner, so the label must stay on the Pressable. |
| Touch targets | 48 pt high buttons, 44 pt icon buttons with `hitSlop` | Apple: 44 × 44 pt. Material 3: 48 × 48 dp. WCAG 2.2 rule 2.5.8: at least 24 px. |
| Android feedback | `android_ripple` with the accent color, `overflow: "hidden"` on rounded controls | Material ripple, clipped to the radius |
| Haptics | None | The app is read-only. Apple asks to use haptics only for their system meaning. |
| `@expo/ui` | Not used | It needs a `Host` wrapper, ignores flexbox inside it, draws the system look over the flat Tesla style, and SDK 58 changed its API. |
| Styling | `StyleSheet.create` for layout, colors from `usePalette()` inline | Expo's color-theme guide shows this pattern, and the React Compiler memoizes the inline objects. NativeWind 5 is a release candidate, and Unistyles adds native packages. |
| Numbers | `fontVariant: ["tabular-nums"]` | The Inter 400 and 500 files of `@expo-google-fonts/inter` 0.4.2 contain the `tnum` feature. React Native passes it on both platforms. |
| List row time column | The start column of `ListRow` is 76 pt wide for `RowTime`. A row with an icon passes `startWidth`. The width does not scale with the text size. | HarfBuzz with the Inter files of the app measures `10:00 AM` (strong, 15 pt) at 71.3 pt and `99 h 59 min` (caption, 12 pt) at 68.4 pt. The old 64 pt column broke both onto two lines. 72 pt leaves 0.7 pt, and Android can round each glyph to a whole pixel, so 76 pt is the next step on the 4 pt grid. A fixed width aligns the clock, the duration, and the titles of all rows without a layout pass. Material 3 lists have a leading slot, a content slot, and a trailing slot, and the leading slot stays narrower than the content. Apple's Larger Text criteria and WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.4 ask for no loss of content when text grows, and a later change handles that with a stacked layout. A width measured from the rows needs a second layout pass, and the titles move after the first frame. A short duration from the server gives the web and the app two texts for one value, and the 12-hour clock still breaks. |
| Long text in rows | The title and the subtitle of `ListRow`, `MenuRow`, and `Records` wrap onto as many lines as the text needs. No row cuts a place name or a summary. Only a short value in the end slot keeps `numberOfLines={1}`. The summary line of `DayList` goes through `summaryLine()`, which puts a no-break space between the words of each part, so the line breaks only between its parts. | HarfBuzz with the Inter files of the app measures a place name of 35 characters at 267 to 272 pt in `strong` (15 pt) and 247 to 252 pt in `label` (14 pt). The text column of a Drives row is 197 pt on a 402 pt phone and 155 pt on a 360 pt phone. On the 402 pt phone, "round trip from NAME · 2 stops, 1 with a charge" needs 4 lines, and a year record "Longest drive: NAME → NAME" needs 4 lines. A limit of two lines still cuts five of eight measured rows. The server puts the place before the stop count and the time, so a cut hid these facts first. Without a cut, nothing hides them. Apple's typography guidance says: "Keep text truncation to a minimum as font size increases", and "Avoid truncating text in scrollable regions unless people can open a separate view to read the rest of the content". Apple's lists guidance asks to preserve text that might otherwise get clipped or truncated, and the Larger Text criteria ask to wrap text to two or more lines instead of truncation. Material 3 list items have one, two, or three lines. WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.10 asks for content without loss of information. In React Native 0.88, `numberOfLines` defaults to 0, which sets no limit. Unicode UAX #14 names the no-break space as the character that keeps two words on the same line. Rejected: a two-line limit, because it still cuts a name of 35 characters. The stop count before the place name, because the web shows the place first, so the app would have a second text for one value, and a cut then hides the place. A shorter Overview summary from the server, because the shortest useful text, "South Mountain Lookout · 20 km · Yesterday 17:05", is 331 pt wide in a 318 pt column and wraps anyway. |
| Large text | Body text scales freely. The hero, display, and title sizes stop at 1.2 times. | A 64 pt number at full Dynamic Type no longer fits a phone. |
| Reduce motion | `AccessibilityInfo.isReduceMotionEnabled()` plus the `reduceMotionChanged` event | Reanimated's hook reads the setting only at start. |
| Loading | The T-mark of the web breathes in the middle of the screen: it fades in, then pulses between full and 35% opacity every 1.4 s. Old data stays on screen with a notice when a refresh fails. | The web shows the same breathing mark in `loading.tsx`, so a screen change looks the same in both. The fade-in hides a short load, as the 400 ms delay of the spinner did. The web keeps the old screen instead of a skeleton (decision 0002). |
| Launch | The native splash is the plain dark background. Then the T-mark draws its outline and fills in 1.1 s, as the web splash does, and the splash fades out after at least 1.15 s. With Reduce Motion, the mark shows filled and the splash goes without a fade. | A static logo in the native splash would jump when the drawing starts. `react-native-svg` has no `pathLength`, so the dash length is the measured outline of the mark, 134 units. |
| Map mount | A map mounts 330 ms after its screen, and the frame shows the card color until then. | Creating the native map during the push animation dropped frames. |
| Pull to refresh | A `refreshing` state of its own, not `isRefetching` | `isRefetching` is true for every background refetch, so the spinner would flash every 30 s. |
| Polling | `subscribed: useIsFocused()` on every query | Tab screens stay mounted. A hidden tab must not poll. |
| Theme | Dark at first. System, Light, or Dark, kept on the phone and set with `Appearance.setColorScheme` before the first screen. | `setColorScheme` overrides the scheme for the app only. React Native 0.88 takes `'auto'` to follow the system again and marks `'unspecified'` as old. The API keeps no choice, so the app stores it. The splash is dark, because the native splash cannot read the stored choice. |

## Charts

A small `react-native-svg` chart (SDK 58 bundles 15.16.0), not a chart library.

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| victory-native 42 | It needs Skia, which adds about 6 MB on iOS and 4 MB on Android. It also computes its own ticks, while the server already gives the ticks and labels in the user's format. |
| react-native-gifted-charts 1.4 | One view per point, and an open issue reports slow rendering at 300 points. |
| @wuba/react-native-echarts | A large JavaScript bundle |

The server sends each chart as points in the user's units, the axis domains, the ticks, and the tooltip labels. The app scales and draws one path for each series, the bars, a faint horizontal grid, and a scrubbing tooltip. A vertical scroll takes over from scrubbing.

A `shares` block draws each bar as a part of the whole, and the first number of the row text gives the same quantity. The note of the panel names it, for example "Share of distance" or "Share of driving time". The web follows the same rule ([share bars](ui-stack.md#insight-pages)).

Since 2026-11-16, `buildChart` on the server and `Chart` and `Heatmap` in the app follow these rules:

| Topic | Rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Count axis | The axis mode `count` steps by 1, 2, or 5 times a power of ten, never below 1, with at most five intervals from zero. Battery "Charge start and end levels" uses it. | A count has no half. Before, the axis showed 2.5 and 7.5. Apple asks to "Prefer familiar sequences of values in the tick and grid-line labels for an axis". |
| Bar labels | A bar chart labels every n-th bar from the first bar. n is the larger of ceil(bars / 5) and the number of bars that the widest label needs with the 8 pt tick gap. Ten level buckets show 0%, 20%, 40%, 60%, and 80%. Five month bars show the first, the third, and the fifth month. | Equal gaps. Before, the labels showed 0%, 20%, 50%, 70%, and 90%. A month label such as `Sep 2026` is about 50 pt wide, and five month bars have about 52 pt each, so five labels touched. |
| Tick gap | The server keeps at least 8 pt between two labels of the horizontal axis. It measures each label against a model of the 390 pt phone: a plot of 262 pt with one value axis and 230 pt with two, and 6.5 pt for each character of an 11 pt label. Day ticks at irregular dates drop a label that comes closer than 8 pt to the label before it. Clock and number ticks keep every n-th tick with the smallest n that fits, so their gaps stay equal. | In Battery "Capacity", the labels `17 Oct` and `22 Oct` touched. Apple asks to tailor the grid lines and labels to the use of a chart: too many overwhelm, and too few make a value hard to estimate. A wider screen, such as an iPad, shows fewer labels than it can hold. |
| Tick label size | The tick labels of `Chart` stop growing at 1.2 times the font scale, the cap of the large text variants. The scrubbing tooltip, the legend, and the caption keep the full scale. | At the text size XXXL, the 64 pt label box cut `Sep 2026` to `Sep 20…`. At 1.2 times, `Sep 2026` is about 60 pt wide, and the tick gap model holds only near the default size. WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.4 lets the platform zoom meet the rule, and the tooltip gives each value at the full text size. |
| Value axis fit | A value axis without a fixed start (`auto`) takes the smallest range of 3, 4, or 5 intervals with a step of 1, 2, or 5 times a power of ten, around the values with 5% padding. It starts at zero or above when no value is below zero. When two ticks get the same label, the fit takes the next larger step until each tick has its own label. A label from 1000 shows hundreds only, such as `2.6k`, so the step there is 100 or more. `fitAxis` in `src/components/charts/nice-domain.ts` gives the domain and the ticks. | Elevation from 265 to 340 m showed an axis from 250 to 450, and the odometer from 2.4k to 4.1k showed 2.0k to 6.0k. Now they show 260 to 360 and 2.0k to 4.5k. Apple asks for "a dynamic range when the possible data values can vary widely and you want the marks to fill the available plot area". Over 200 000 random ranges, the fit without the label rule uses 69% of the axis on average, against 51% before, and 45.5% in the worst case, against 25%. A step of 50 gave the odometer from 2606 to 2744 km the labels `2.6k 2.6k 2.7k 2.7k 2.8k 2.8k`. Now it shows `2.5k 2.6k 2.7k 2.8k`. Over 20 000 random odometer ranges with a spread of 50 to 600 km, no label repeats, against 18% with the axis before. The values then use 61% of the axis on average. A small spread above 1000, such as 5 m of elevation at 2000 m, shows an almost flat line, because the step is 100 or more. A step of 2.5 fits only a little better and gives labels such as `437.5`, which do not fit the 34 pt label box. The web keeps `niceDomain`, because Recharts always draws five ticks. |
| Caption | A chart can have a `caption`. The app shows it above the legend. The two charts of Battery "Projected range" have `By outside temperature (°C)` and `By battery level (%)`, the headings of the web. | A person could not tell what each of the two charts compares. Apple asks for descriptive titles on a chart and for consistent text for several charts of one dataset. The caption names the horizontal axis and its unit. An older app ignores the new field, and a new block kind would break it. An older app draws every `note` block centered with padding, so a note does not read as a heading there. |
| Line styles | A series can have a `dash`: `solid`, `dashed`, `dotted`, or `dashDot`. The four tires of "Tire pressure" have the four styles. The first DC curve of Charging insights is solid, and each later curve has a pattern. The patterns are 6 pt on and 8 pt off, 1 pt on and 8 pt off, and 8, 8, 1, and 8 pt. The round cap of the 2 pt line adds 1 pt to each end of a dash, so the visible gaps are 6 pt wide. When a series of a chart has a pattern, the legend shows a 22 pt line sample in the style of each series instead of the dot. | Equal values hid lines. The front tires lay under the rear right tire, and three demo DC curves showed as one line. The first line lies at the bottom and stays solid, and each later line has gaps, so the lines below show through. Apple says "Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate between different pieces of data", and lets a legend describe the use of color or shape. WCAG 2.2 technique G111 uses color and pattern together for rule 1.4.1. An offset was rejected, because it changes the data. A style by series index in the app was rejected, because it changes every chart with two series. `react-native-svg` 15.16.0 has `strokeDasharray`. |
| Heatmap key | A row under the hour labels of `Heatmap`: `0`, an empty cell, four cells from light to full blue, and the largest count of one hour. A screen reader reads the row as one element: "Darker cells mean more drives. The most in one hour: 4." | "When the car drives" and "When the car charges" had no key for the shades of blue. Apple asks for descriptive content that explains the visual characteristics of a chart. The key uses the same opacity rule as the cells. |
| Month label | A month has the full year, for example `Aug 2026`. The ISO date order keeps `2026-08`. | `Aug 26` reads as 26 August. A period of one year or more crosses a year edge, so the month alone is not enough. |
| Unit on the horizontal axis | A chart with `xUnit` shows the unit on each tick, for example `25 °C`. | Each other axis has its unit in the panel title, in a percent tick, or in the legend. Apple lets a title carry the unit. |
| Current of a charge | The blue `chart1`, against the amber voltage. Before, it had the gray `chart3`. | The current of an AC charge is a flat line, and a gray flat line looks like a grid line. On the card, the blue has 4.39:1 in light and 4.72:1 in dark, above the 3:1 of WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.11 for a chart line. The gray had 2.98:1 in light. Blue and amber is the pair of the palette that is safest for color vision deficiency. The legend names each line, so the color is not the only signal (rule 1.4.1). |
| "Usable" line of Battery | The amber `chart4`, as on the web | Apple asks to avoid the same color for different meanings, so the app and the web agree. |

The gray `chart3` stays on bars and areas, such as the "Start" bars and the elevation, because they do not read as grid lines. The front right tire keeps the gray, and its dashed line does not read as a grid line. Every series color differs from the grid color `border`. Material Design 3 has no chart guidance.

The web keeps solid lines and draws no heatmap key. An older server sends no `caption` and no `dash`, and the app then draws solid lines and no caption.
