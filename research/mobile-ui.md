# My Tesla mobile UI research

Research date: 2026-10-17. Checked against Expo SDK 58 (React Native 0.88, React 19.3, expo-router 58.0.23), the Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Material Design 3, WCAG 2.2, and the package registries.

## Question

Which navigation, components, charts, and maps give a native iOS and Android app that follows the Tesla style of the web dashboard, stays small, and is accessible?

## Navigation

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Tabs | Five native tabs: Overview, Drives, Charging, Insights, Settings | Apple asks for the minimum number of tabs and, in general, three to five on iPhone. Material 3 and the Android navigation bar allow three to five destinations, and Android native tabs allow at most five. Battery, Stats, and Places already share one period control on the web, so they form Insights. |
| Settings | A tab, not a modal | Settings now changes the web Settings, with a picker for each choice. A tab keeps that stack of screens, and the gear button on Overview is gone. Five is the limit, so a new destination needs a decision record. |
| Choice lists | A pushed screen with one row for each option and a check mark on the chosen one. It stays open after a choice. | The Settings pattern of iOS: the check mark moves at once, and Back returns. A choice saves at once, with the old value back when the server refuses it. |
| Settings groups | The server names each group of the app after the screen that its settings change: "Insights" holds the default period, "Overview" holds "Car image" and "Last drive and charge", and "Status and Location" holds "Live status", "Today", "Tires", and "Location". The web keeps one group "Overview", because its Overview page holds all the cards. The app and the web share the keys and the labels. | Android says "Group settings in smaller relevant groups" with headings between them, and asks for settings that are "well-organized, predictable". Apple groups related settings, and a grouped list separates its groups with headers. WCAG 2.4.6 asks that headings "describe topic or purpose". One group "Overview and Status" did not name the Location screen. The label "Location" repeats a word of its group title, which Android advises against. It stays the same as the web label. |
| Search in a long list | `Stack.SearchBar` on a choice list with more than 12 options, such as the time zone | The native search field of the header. SDK 55 and later replace `headerSearchBarOptions` with this component. |
| Stacks | A `Stack` inside each tab folder, with `unstable_settings = { anchor: "index" }` | `initialRouteName` is gone in SDK 58. The anchor puts the list under a deep link, so Back returns to it. |
| Status bar on a screen without a header | `Screen withoutHeader` draws an opaque strip in the page background over the top safe-area inset, on iOS and on Android. The strip ignores touches and comes after the ScrollView, so the ScrollView stays the first child. On Android, the content also starts below the inset. The Overview on a phone and the two states of `CarProvider` use it. Connect draws its own ScrollView and renders the same strip with `StatusBarStrip`. The Overview on an iPad has a navigation bar and passes `coverStatusBar`: `Screen` draws the same strip where the screen lies under the bar, which `headerCoversScreen()` decides (iOS 26 and later for a bar without a large title). | On iPadOS 27 the navigation bar of the Overview did not hide the content under the status bar. At the text size `accessibility-large` in light mode, the car name and the time of the last drive showed through the clock and the date. Below iOS 26 that bar is opaque and the screen starts below it, so a strip there would cover the top of the content. Apple says: "Obscure content under the status bar." Apple prefers a scroll edge effect, and iOS 27 adds its automatic soft effect to the Overview, but the blur still showed the dark car behind the clock in light mode. The hard style of `UIScrollEdgeEffect` needs iOS 26, and the app supports iOS 16.4 and Android, so the app needs a strip in any case. `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior` works on iOS only. Android 15 and later draw every app edge to edge, so the first row of the Overview sat under the status bar on an Android 16 emulator until the inset was added. Android protects the status bar with the background color of the pane. With the strip, the status bar text always sits on the page background, so its contrast does not depend on the content (WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.3). The car name stays directly below the status bar, as [decision 0025](../docs/decisions/0025-the-tesla-app-layout.md) shows it. A solid strip needs no gradient, so the stage glow stays the only one. Rejected: a navigation bar on the phone, because it moves the name down by about 53 pt. The hard scroll edge effect, because it needs iOS 26 and still blurs. |
| Overview header on the iPad | On an iPad, the `index` of the Overview stack keeps its navigation bar (`overviewHeaderShown` in `src/navigation/header.ts`). On iPadOS 27 the bar sits behind the top tab bar and shows no title. The car name stays below the tab bar in every state. The bar does not hide the content under the status bar, so the Overview passes `coverStatusBar` and gets the status bar strip of the row above. The phone and Android keep the Overview without a header. | The car name went under the tab bar after a return from Status or Location, by the tab or by the back button. The Overview stack hid the `UINavigationBar` for `index` and showed it for the detail screens. After the bar hid again, UIKit gave the scroll view a top inset of the status bar only, and a second press, a tab switch, or a pull to refresh kept it. Drives keeps its bar on the root screen and did not have the problem. When the bar never hides in the stack, UIKit keeps the inset of the tab bar, as on the other four tabs. The name now sits 10 pt higher than before at rest, and at the same place after each return. Apple puts the iPad tab bar near the top of the screen. Apple forum reports show that the safe area of iOS 26 can go stale after a bar change. Rejected: a manual inset for the tab bar, because JavaScript cannot read the inset after UIKit loses it. |
| Active tab pressed again | Native tabs pop the stack, or scroll the root screen to the top. On iOS, `Screen` completes the scroll with `useScrollToRest()` in `src/hooks/`. The hook keeps the offset of the first drag after mount that starts below zero, which is the offset at rest with the large title at full size. An offset of zero or more means that the page moved before the first drag, by VoiceOver, a hardware keyboard, or the keyboard inset, so the hook keeps no offset and does nothing. When the active tab is pressed again, `useScrollToTop` of `expo-router` calls the hook, and the hook scrolls to that offset. The ScrollView sets `scrollToOverflowEnabled`, because the offset at rest is negative. | `rnscreens_scrollToTop` of `react-native-screens` 4.30 scrolls to minus `adjustedContentInset.top`. With a collapsed large title, that inset is the height of the small bar, so the scroll stopped there. The title stayed small on Drives, Charging, Insights, and Settings, and on the iPad it disappeared. A programmatic scroll does not bring a large title back on its own (Apple forum). JavaScript cannot read `adjustedContentInset`, and `scrollTo` clamps a negative offset unless `scrollToOverflowEnabled` is on. React Navigation documents a wrapped ref with its own `scrollToTop` for a custom offset. `useScrollToTop` scrolls only a focused screen that is the first route of its stack, so a first press on another tab and a pop do not scroll. Before the first drag the page is at rest, so the hook does nothing. Android has no large title and its native tabs scroll to the top, so the hook does nothing there. Limit: a change of the text size while a screen is mounted changes the height of the large title, and a resize of an iPad window between the top tab bar and the bottom tab bar changes the top inset. In both cases the kept offset is wrong until the screen mounts again. Rejected: a patch of `rnscreens_scrollToTop`, because a native patch needs upkeep and UIKit has no public value for the height of the large bar. A remount of the ScrollView, because it loses the state and has no animation. |
| Headers | Native large titles on the list screens, a small title on the detail screens | The ScrollView must be the first child for the large title to collapse. iOS 26 makes headers transparent with Liquid Glass by default, so the app adds no blur. |
| Header fonts | `headerTitleStyle` and `headerLargeTitleStyle` set Inter | iOS 26 headers ignore the theme fonts in SDK 58. |
| Lists | A ScrollView with sticky day headings | Native tabs scroll a ScrollView to the top and pop the stack when the active tab is pressed again. They do not do this for a FlatList. On iOS, `Screen` completes the scroll, so a large title returns to its full size (see "Active tab pressed again"). A list holds at most 50 rows. |
| Day headings under the bar | Drives, Charging, and the Report render through `DayList`. Where the navigation bar covers the list, `DayList` gives the ScrollView `StickyHeading` as its `StickyHeaderComponent`. `StickyHeading` does the interpolation of the React Native wrapper on the scroll offset plus `useAnimatedHeaderHeight()` of `expo-router/native-stack`, so the pinned heading sits at the lower edge of the bar. `headerCoversScreen()` in `sticky-geometry.ts` is true on iOS 26 and later, and for a large title on an older iOS. Elsewhere the ScrollView keeps the default wrapper. The heading keeps the page background of `SectionHeading`, so the rows do not show through it. | `Screen` sets `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior="automatic"`, so UIKit adds the bar height as an adjusted inset, and the offset at rest is minus the bar height. The default wrapper (`ScrollViewStickyHeader`) starts to pin a heading when the offset reaches the layout `y` of the heading, which is the top of the scroll view frame. `ScrollView` adds only the `contentInset` prop to its scroll value. On iOS 26 and later, `react-native-screens` 4.30 always lays a screen out under the bar (`screensExtendUnderOpaqueNavigationBar`). Before iOS 26 it does this only for a translucent header, and a large title makes the header translucent. So the heading stuck behind the status bar and the bar, about 116 pt too high on an iPhone 17 Pro. The animated header height follows the bar on the native thread when the large title collapses: `RNSScreen.mm` computes it in `calculateHeaderHeightIsModal`. The scroll value is 0 until the first scroll event, while the true offset is minus the bar height, so `StickyHeading` pins nothing before that event. The heading is one line, about 36 pt at the default size, and a row is at least 64 pt high, so a pinned heading never hides a focused row completely (WCAG 2.2 rule 2.4.11). A pinned heading follows the finger and is not an animation, so Reduce Motion does not change it. Apple asks for a clear separation for pinned table headers under the scroll edge effect, and Android pins the headers of grouped data too. The web pins `DayHeading` under its header with the page background. The Report put its headings in a nested view before, so they did not stick. Rejected: an opaque header, because the large title needs a translucent header and the Liquid Glass bar would go away on these screens only. A fixed `contentInset` with `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior="never"`, because it breaks the collapse of the large title and the tab bar inset. A wrapper around `ScrollViewStickyHeader`, because it needs a deep import from `react-native/Libraries`, which has no types in React Native 0.88. `useHeaderHeight()`, because expo-router delays the number by 100 ms on a large title screen, so the heading sticks 52 pt too low for a moment after a fast scroll. A second heading drawn from JavaScript scroll events, because it lags by a frame and has no push between two headings. `SectionList` or FlashList, because they use the same wrapper, and native tabs do not scroll them to the top. |
| Day total in the heading | A day heading of Drives, Charging, and the Report shows the date and the total of the day in one line: "Wed 21 Oct · 4 trips · 64 km", "Tue 20 Oct · +18.9 kWh", or both parts on a Report day with trips and charges. The server sends the parts in `total` of each section, in the units of the person, and `headingLine()` joins them after the date with `summaryLine()`. The heading keeps the style, the header role, and the one text element of `SectionHeading`. With an older server, `total` is absent and the heading shows only the date. | Apple asks for a header that gives the context of a list and for short item text that does not truncate, and puts the most important information first, so the date stays first and each part stays short (Apple HIG lists and tables, writing). WCAG 2.2 rule 2.4.6 asks that a heading describes its topic and says that a heading does not need to be long. `summaryLine()` keeps the words of each part together, so at a large text size the heading wraps only between parts and never cuts (WCAG 2.2 rules 1.4.4 and 1.4.10, Apple Larger Text). The web shows the same parts through `DotList`, and both read them from `src/lib/day-total.ts`. `total` is optional in the contract because the app and a self-hosted server have separate releases, and `listShape` stays loose for the same reason. A Report day counts only its rows, so a charge inside a trip does not add to the energy part. Rejected: the total inside `title`, because one text can break "64" from "km" and the React keys use `title`. A right-aligned total, because it needs a second style and a layout that fails at a large text size. A cost in the Charging heading, because the summary line of the list shows the cost and the heading stays short. |
| Long list in a panel | The lock history shows 5 rows and a secondary **Show N more** button that extends the list in place; **Show fewer** folds it back | Apple and Material both prefer progressive disclosure over a menu for more of the same rows, and the rows stay in the scroll view, so VoiceOver reads them in order. The web uses a native `details` element for the same panel. |
| Overview structure | The car on a stage with callouts, the name with a chevron that switches the car, a row of quick actions, then a menu of rows with an icon, a title, and a short summary. On a wide screen the menu is a sidebar on the left and the car on the right. The right pane is as high as the sidebar, the stage fills the pane above the quick actions, and the callouts and the car sit in its center. Since 2026-11-16, the Location row follows `location`, as the Status row follows the MQTT feed: with the card "Location" or the maps off, the menu has no Location row and the quick actions have no "Maps". The other rows keep their order. Since 2026-11-16, the Status row has no summary when the Live card is off: the switch "Live status" is off, or the car has no records. The row keeps its place, icon, and title. | Ege asked for the layout of Ian Zelbo's Tesla app concept (2026-10-22). The Tesla app itself stacks the car, the quick actions, and a list of sections on a phone; Apple's sidebar guidance puts a persistent sidebar on the leading edge of a wide window and a stacked layout in a compact width, and Material 3 uses a navigation rail or drawer at 840 dp and wider. The rows follow the Material 3 two-line list item (leading icon, headline, supporting text, 64 dp min height) and Apple's sidebar row (icon and title, the selected row filled). The Location row hides with its content, because Apple asks to hide unavailable context menu items, not to dim them, and a person expects the app to follow the Settings. Apple keeps the tab bar buttons and the items of a standard menu, but those are fixed navigation and commands. The five tabs do not change. WCAG 2.2 rule 3.2.3 asks for the same relative order of repeated navigation, and the other rows keep it. A dimmed row was rejected: `MenuRow` has no off state, and the person turned the content off. The Status row keeps a title without a summary, because the Status screen still holds Today, the tires, and the car figures. Apple asks for succinct item text and allows a list of item titles only, where the detail view shows the content. The Settings row already has a title only. The odometer as the summary was rejected, because the right callout shows it on most days and each value has one place (decision 0025, point 7). A summary from Today, the tires, or the temperatures was rejected: it needs new text rules and changes with three other switches. |
| Callouts | A small uppercase label over a bold value, three at most, with no line. The callouts sit in the layout flow above the car: the top callout in the center, then the left and the right callout in one row at the 20 pt screen margin. The row wraps, so at a large text size the right callout moves under the left one. The label uses `mutedForeground`. A value has no line limit and wraps. Values come formatted from the server. Since [decision 0028](../docs/decisions/0028-the-car-fills-the-stage.md). Content since 2026-11-16: the battery and the range on the left, with the warning tone at 20% or less; the lock and Sentry Mode on top, only with the MQTT feed; the power and the limit of a charge on the right, else the charge port, else the odometer. Without the feed the stage has two callouts. | The concept's callouts name a part and its state. Three keep the stage readable at 390 pt. The lines of 24 pt stopped in empty space, and the render has no fixed anchor points across models, so a line cannot reach a part of the car ([decision 0025](../docs/decisions/0025-the-tesla-app-layout.md)). Apple keeps content inside the system margins, and every other element of the Overview keeps 20 pt, so the callouts do too. The label with `subtle` had 3.3:1 in light and 3.8:1 in dark, below the 4.5:1 of WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.3 for 12 pt text. `mutedForeground` gives 6.5:1 in light and 7.4:1 in dark. Text in the flow grows the stage and never covers the car, and the label breaks only between words (WCAG 2.2 rules 1.4.4 and 1.4.10). A screen reader reads each callout as one element. The "Last seen" callout repeated the age in the title, and the word "Charging" as a value repeated the state, so both went away. The low battery glyph of the title went away too, so the left callout carries the low warning. The value is always text, so the tone never carries the meaning alone (WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.1). |
| One place for each value | Each fact of the Overview shows in one place: the state and the age in the title, the battery and the range in the left callout, the lock on top, the charge or the odometer on the right. A menu summary gives a fact of the screen that it opens and that the stage does not show: the last drive, the last charge, the cold battery, the health and the capacity, the distance and the efficiency of the default period, the count of the visited places in the default period (since 2026-11-16), the live values that the stage does not show. The server builds the summaries, and a test checks that no part of the title, the callouts, and the summaries repeats. A place name is the exception (since 2026-11-16): the end of the last drive, the place of the last charge, and the place of a parked car are three facts that can share a place, and a test covers a car that is parked where it charged last. [Decision 0025](../docs/decisions/0025-the-tesla-app-layout.md) point 7 has the table. | The battery showed three times and the odometer two times. Apple's layout guidance says: "An interface with too much content and too many choices makes it harder to find information quickly". Apple's writing guidance says: "Check each word to be sure it needs to be there." and asks to "put the most important information first". Apple's lists guidance says: "Keep item text succinct so row content is comfortable to read." Material 3 gives a two-line list item a headline and supporting text, so the summary supports the title with its own fact. The Battery and Stats summaries use the same helpers as the pages that they open, so the numbers match. The Stats summary uses the default period of the person, because the Stats page has no calendar month. Rejected: the battery in the title and not in the callout, because the callout keeps the level and the range together under a label. |

| Unit on the horizontal axis | A chart with `xUnit` shows the unit on each tick, for example `25 °C`. | Each other axis has its unit in the panel title, in a percent tick, or in the legend. Apple lets a title carry the unit. |
| Current of a charge | The blue `chart1`, against the amber voltage. Before, it had the gray `chart3`. | The current of an AC charge is a flat line, and a gray flat line looks like a grid line. On the card, the blue has 4.39:1 in light and 4.72:1 in dark, above the 3:1 of WCAG 2.2 rule 1.4.11 for a chart line. The gray had 2.98:1 in light. Blue and amber is the pair of the palette that is safest for color vision deficiency. The legend names each line, so the color is not the only signal (rule 1.4.1). |
| "Usable" line of Battery | The amber `chart4`, as on the web | Apple asks to avoid the same color for different meanings, so the app and the web agree. |

The gray `chart3` stays on bars and areas, such as the "Start" bars and the elevation, because they do not read as grid lines. The front right tire keeps the gray, and its dashed line does not read as a grid line. Every series color differs from the grid color `border`. Material Design 3 has no chart guidance.

The web keeps solid lines and draws no heatmap key. An older server sends no `caption` and no `dash`, and the app then draws solid lines and no caption.

## Maps

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | `@maplibre/maplibre-react-native` 11.5.0 with the CARTO Positron and Dark Matter styles | The engine and the styles of the web, so the maps look the same. Only CARTO sees the tile requests. `expo-maps` and `react-native-maps` would send them to Apple or Google, and Android would need a Google key. Version 11 runs on the new architecture only and has an Expo config plugin. Checked on React Native 0.88 in a development build on the iOS 27 simulator on 2026-10-18. |
| Layout | Edge to edge, square corners, outside the cards. A drive and a trip open with the map under the navigation bar. Height: 46% of the screen for a route, Places, and Location, 38% for the place of a charge, between 260 and 560 pt. Location shows the place name and the parked time above its map, and the age of the position and "Open in Maps" under it. Since 2026-11-16, the map of Location has 46%. | Apple's layout guidance asks to extend visual content to the edges of the display and keep controls in the safe area. The attribution button keeps the screen margin. A map in a card looked like a thumbnail. |
| Gestures | No pan, rotate, or tilt inside a scrolling screen. Pinch and double tap zoom. | One finger always scrolls the screen, as the cooperative gestures of the web do. Pinch zoom zooms at the fingers, so it separates the dots of one city. Apple says "In general, make your map interactive". Decided on 2026-11-16 and not built yet: a press on the map of Places, the Report, a drive, a trip, and "Every road driven" opens the same map on its own screen of the same tab stack. One finger pans there, and the native back button closes it. The screen reads its data again from the same query hook, so no coordinate goes through a route parameter. The stack turns `fullScreenGestureEnabled` off for it, as for the Timeline, because the full-screen back swipe of iOS 26 and later takes the pan. Apple also asks to cluster overlapping points, which is an option for the place dots there. Rejected: a modal for the full-screen map, because it hides the tab bar and needs its own close button. |
| Route under the labels | `RouteMap` puts the line layer `route-line` before the layer `labelLayer` (`watername_ocean`) with `beforeId`. The line lies above the roads, the buildings, and the borders, and under the names of places, roads, and water. The marker dots stay on top. The web does the same for the drive, trip, and Report maps. Since 2026-11-16, [decision 0038](../docs/decisions/0038-value-notes-route-under-labels-and-back-from-a-quick-action.md). | The route line covered the city names of the Report. MapLibre draws the layers in the order of the style, and a layer without `beforeId` goes on top of all of them. Apple names "Above roads" as the default level of an overlay, and "Above labels" hides everything beneath it. Checked on 2026-11-16: Dark Matter and Positron have 95 layers each, `watername_ocean` is the 69th in both, and the 27 layers from there to the end are all `symbol` layers. The only label layer before it is `waterway_label`, the names along rivers. Known limit: the native code of `@maplibre/maplibre-react-native` 11.5.0 waits for the layer of `beforeId`. If CARTO removes `watername_ocean`, the route line does not draw in the app until the constant changes. The web checks the layer and then draws the line on top. Rejected: a check in the app, because it needs a second download of the style in JavaScript and a prop change on a mounted layer. `layerIndex`, because the number of layers can change with each style version. |
| Markers | Routes and dots are `Layer`s on a `GeoJSONSource`. The car is a `Marker` view that pulses, and it stops with Reduce Motion. | Style layers keep a map with many places fast. The dot colors and sizes copy the web: circle radius plus stroke equals the web size. |
| Ornaments | The attribution stays, at the bottom right. No logo and no compass. | OpenStreetMap and CARTO need the attribution. The compass has no use without rotation. |
| CARTO key | None | The vector styles of the web answer without a key on 2026-10-18. CARTO asks for a free key for raster tiles, and says that vector tiles come next. |
